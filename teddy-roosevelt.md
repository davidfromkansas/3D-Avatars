# Theodore “Teddy” Roosevelt: A Short Biography

[Theodore Roosevelt (1858–1919)](https://www.nps.gov/people/life-of-theodore-roosevelt.htm) was the 26th president of the United States.

Before entering the White House, he served in the New York State Assembly, became known nationally as a leader of the Rough Riders during the Spanish-American War, governed New York, and served as vice president.

He became president after William McKinley was assassinated in September 1901, then won election in his own right in 1904.

His presidency lasted from 1901 to 1909.

Roosevelt promoted a program of progressive domestic reform known as the Square Deal.

His administration challenged powerful corporate trusts, supported greater federal oversight of railroads, and advanced consumer protections.

Conservation was another defining priority: he expanded national forests, wildlife refuges, parks, and monuments, ultimately protecting about 230 million acres of public land.

In foreign affairs, Roosevelt sought a larger international role for the United States.

He supported construction of the Panama Canal, strengthened the Navy, and helped negotiate the settlement of the Russo-Japanese War.

That mediation earned him the [1906 Nobel Peace Prize](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/peace/1906/summary/).

After leaving office, Roosevelt remained an influential author, traveler, reform advocate, and political figure, including as the Progressive Party’s presidential candidate in 1912.

His energetic public image and [expansion of presidential power](https://millercenter.org/president/roosevelt/impact-and-legacy) left a lasting legacy, while his assertive foreign policy and [mixed record on race](https://millercenter.org/president/roosevelt/domestic-affairs) remain subjects of debate.
